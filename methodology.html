Scoring methodology

Eight categories. Five maturity levels.

Every score is derived from how your answers map to eight operational categories. Here is exactly how the calculation works and what each result means.

Take the assessment →
Assessment structure
27 Total questions Across 9 workflow sections. Most questions are scored 1–4 based on your answer.
8 Categories scored Each question maps to one or more categories. Some questions count toward three categories simultaneously.
2 Open text questions Captured for context and used in your downloadable report. They do not affect your numerical score.
5 Maturity profiles Your overall percentage score maps to one of five operational states, each with a specific priority set.
How the score is calculated
Step 01
Answer scoring
Each multiple-choice question is scored 1–4. A score of 4 indicates a strong, mature workflow. A score of 1 indicates a reactive or invisible process. The exact scoring reflects the operational consequence of each answer — not all 1s and 4s are equal in weight across categories.
Step 02
Category aggregation
Each question maps to one, two, or three categories. Your category score is the sum of earned points divided by the maximum possible points for that category, expressed as a percentage. A category with fewer contributing questions is not penalised — the percentage normalises it.
Step 03
Overall maturity score
Your overall score is the total earned points across all scored questions divided by the total possible points — giving a single percentage that drives your maturity profile. The five profiles have fixed score thresholds: 0%, 45%, 62%, 78%, and 90%.
What we measure
01
Visibility
Can leadership see what is really happening before it becomes a surprise? Covers pipeline clarity, job status, and real-time operational awareness.
Contributes to 7 questions
02
Standardization
Do teams follow a consistent process, or does every person run their own version? Covers proposal templates, intake workflows, and process variability.
Contributes to 9 questions
03
Accountability
Is ownership clear when a task, deal, handoff, or follow-up stalls? Covers escalation paths, ownership of overdue work, and pipeline accuracy.
Contributes to 5 questions
04
Scalability
Can the process grow without relying on hero employees and tribal knowledge? Covers documentation, automation readiness, and process independence from individuals.
Contributes to 8 questions
05
Forecast Reliability
Can revenue and workload be predicted with enough confidence to plan? Covers forecast accuracy, visibility window, and the causes of forecast misses.
Contributes to 5 questions
06
Operational Readiness
Can operations execute cleanly on what sales promised? Covers sales-to-ops handoff quality, scope documentation, and pre-close ops involvement.
Contributes to 6 questions
07
Knowledge Retention
Does context survive turnover, vacations, overload, and handoffs? Covers where decisions are stored, how easy history is to access, and knowledge portability.
Contributes to 6 questions
08
Customer Experience
Does the process create trust, clarity, and speed for customers? Covers friction points, repeated information requests, and consistency across reps.
Contributes to 5 questions
What your score means
0 – 44%
Reactive & Tribal
The workflow depends heavily on people remembering, chasing, and interpreting context. Visibility is limited, ownership is inconsistent, and leadership typically sees problems after they have already affected customers or operations.
Where to start
Make ownership explicit
Create one source of truth for active jobs
Document the sales-to-ops handoff
45 – 61%
Growing but Fragmented
The business has working habits, but they vary by person, team, or project. Some information is visible, some lives in inboxes and spreadsheets, and follow-up depends too much on individual discipline.
Where to start
Standardize the highest-friction workflow
Define escalation rules for stalled tasks
Clean up lead and proposal tracking
62 – 77%
Structured but Siloed
The core process exists, but information still gets trapped between departments, systems, or roles. Forecasting, customer history, and operational readiness may look fine until a handoff gets tested.
Where to start
Tighten sales-to-ops handoff requirements
Connect decision history to pipeline stages
Measure forecast misses by cause
78 – 89%
Connected Operations
The workflow is mostly visible and repeatable. The biggest opportunity is reducing remaining exceptions, improving forecast confidence, and turning strong individual habits into a scalable operating system.
Where to start
Reduce process exceptions
Improve cross-team reporting
Automate the cleanest repetitive workflows
90 – 100%
Operationally Mature
The process is visible, owned, and repeatable. Leadership can see reality, teams know what happens next, and customer experience is less dependent on individual heroics.
Where to start
Benchmark leading indicators
Automate low-risk admin tasks
Use workflow data to coach and plan capacity
